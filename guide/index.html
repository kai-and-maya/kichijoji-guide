Words from the bar counter

Point at the Japanese, or say it out loud. The owners will smile either way.

This night

Ikitsuke行きつけ

Your regular place, where the owner knows your name.

Joren常連

A regular. Tonight, that's us, and maybe one day, you.

Hashigoはしご

Hopping from one small place to the next, like climbing a ladder.

Tachinomi立ち飲み

A standing bar for a quick, casual drink. Some have a few seats, but most people stand.

Walking in

Irasshaimaseいらっしゃいませ

"Welcome!" No need to answer. A smile and a nod is enough.

Oshiboriおしぼり

A hot or cold towel to wipe your hands before you eat.

Otoshiお通し

A small dish that arrives without ordering. It is charged like a small seat charge. That is normal, so no need to send it back.

Ordering

Osusumeおすすめ

"What do you recommend?" Ask the owner, then let them choose.

Omakaseおまかせ

"I'll leave it to you." The owner picks for you.

Okawariおかわり

"Another one, please." A refill of the same drink.

Chaserチェイサー

A glass of water on the side. Just ask for one.

Drinks

Nama生

Draft beer. "Nama, kudasai" gets you one.

Highballハイボール

Whisky and soda. A favorite in bars across Japan.

Hiya / Atsukan冷や・熱燗

Sake at room temperature, or served warm.

Shochu焼酎

A spirit made from sweet potato, barley, or rice. Try it with water or soda.

Umeshu梅酒

Sweet plum liqueur, often on the rocks.

Food

Tsumamiつまみ

Small dishes to go with your drinks.

Obanzaiおばんざい

Kyoto-style home cooking, served in small bowls.

Shime締め

The last dish of the night, often noodles or rice.

The regulars' way

Kanpai乾杯

Cheers. Wait until everyone has a glass before the first sip.

Okaikeiお会計

"The bill, please."

Gochisosamaごちそうさま

Said as you leave. Thanks for the food and drink.

Streets & alleys

Dori通り

Street. Inokashira-dori is Inokashira Street.

Kaido街道

An old road between towns, now a main road. Like Itsukaichi-kaido.

Yokocho横丁

A narrow alley packed with tiny bars and eateries, like Harmonica Yokocho.

Shotengai商店街

A local shopping street, often under a roof. Sunroad is one.

Ura裏

"Behind." Locals call the bar-filled lanes behind a big store by its name, like Yodobashi-ura.

Koen-guchi公園口

The park-side (south) exit of a station. Many izakaya gather around it.

Good manners at the counter

What regulars do.

About tipping

No tips, pleaseチップは不要

Japan has no tipping. Foreign cash can't be used in the shop and is hard to exchange, so a kind tip can leave the owner with a problem.

"Masuta mo ippai dozo"マスターも一杯どうぞ

Want to say thank you? Buy the owner a drink. It goes on your bill like any other.

"Oishikatta"おいしかった

"That was delicious." Owners remember the guests who say it.

At the counter

Drinks firstまず飲み物

Order a drink first, then a few dishes at a time. More can always follow.

Ask before photos写真は一声かけて

Food is usually fine. Ask before photographing the owner or other guests: "Shashin, ii desu ka?"

Shared spaceカウンターは共有

Counter seats are close together. Keep voices easy and bags under your chair.

Smokingたばこ

Rules differ by place, and many are smoke-free inside. Ask before you light up.

Payingお支払い

Many places take cards. A little yen in your pocket still helps.

Sharing the table

Torizara取り皿

Small plates for sharing. Dishes are meant for the table: "Torizara, kudasai."

Oshakuお酌

Pour for your friends, and they pour for you. Nobody fills their own glass first.

Chopsticksお箸

Never pass food from chopsticks to chopsticks, or stand them up in rice.

Last orderラストオーダー

The last call for food and drinks, often 30 minutes before closing.

Small shops, small teams

  • Many of our shops are run by just one or two people. When it's busy, food can take a while. That's part of a small shop, not bad service.
  • Order a few dishes at once, rather than one at a time.
  • If someone is busy cooking, ask the person serving instead. If the owner is doing both, wait for a quiet moment.

Food allergies
